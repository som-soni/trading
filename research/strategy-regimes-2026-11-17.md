# Strategies by market regime (2026-11-17)

Which of the strategies here held up when the market fell, and which led the way back? Every stored backtest's
daily equity, split by what the index was doing. Produced by `swing_screener/backtesting/regime_breakdown.py`
(stored runs only; re-run it after the queued backtests finish — several rows below are in-sample runs that stop at
2021 and miss the 2022 and 2025 declines).

**In short**

- **Nothing wins in every phase.** The strategies that made money while the index fell — trend following across
  assets (`turtle_trend`), trend-timed bonds and gold, the short books — gave back far more in advances and
  recoveries. They are hedges for a sleeve, not a book to switch into.
- **The breakout strategies defend by going to cash.** Minervini, `high52_vcp` and `breakout` lost 41–46% of the
  US index's declines and about 16–20% of India's, holding a position on only about 40% of decline sessions: the
  market filter. That is what is happening in the US now.
- **Momentum falls further than the index and leads the rebound.** US `quality_momentum` lost 131% of the index's
  declines but beat it by 40 points a year in recoveries; India `momentum_baseline` 123% and +7.
- **The ₹5L book, `momentum_dual_combo` (India), lost about two-thirds of each decline** (67% capture over nine;
  −24% in 2020 vs −38%) and kept pace in recoveries. Expect that, not protection.
- **A 200-day switch is too slow for crashes.** The average lags: the 2020 crash fell mostly in "transition" and
  the rebound in "bear", so "go defensive when the index is below a falling 200-day" sells the low.

## How to read it

Two lenses on the same curves (rules in the module's docstring):

1. **Dated phases** — the index's declines of 10% or more, dated after the fact: *decline* (closing high to the
   lowest close), *recovery* (to a new closing high), *advance* (the rest). Hindsight only dates the fall; it is
   the answer to "what worked in a decline". **Capture** = the strategy's summed peak-to-trough returns over every
   decline its curve covers, as a share of the index's. 50% = lost half as much; negative = made money.
2. **The 200-day signal**, as a trader saw it each morning (previous close above/below a rising/falling 200-day).
   It was fixed before any figure; it turned out to describe the signal's lag more than the market (above).

Which run: the full-period run (2013 → today, current rules) where stored; else the best configuration's in-sample
run joined to its out-of-sample run; else the one run there is (**opti** = in-sample only, to 2021).

What it says about the regimes asked about:

- **Bear (a decline under way):** the only books that made money were trend following across asset classes and
  shorts — the published finding too (trend following's "crisis alpha" in 2008 and 2022). Here `turtle_trend` made
  +10%, +26% and +16% in the 2018, 2020 and 2022 US declines; `stage4_shorts` was positive in every India decline.
  Their cost: roughly 15–30 points a year behind the index the rest of the time.
- **Transition (the recovery off a low):** momentum and quality lead, as do trend-exit books that get back in fast
  (`masonson_tqqq`). Defensive books lag badly here — the decline winners lost 30–60 points a year in recoveries.
- **Dual momentum is not a crash hedge.** It checks monthly, so it rode most of the 2020 crash down, and in 2022 its
  bond leg fell with stocks (US −29% vs the index −25%). It helped in the slower 2025 decline (+1.6%).

## Current state (stored data to 2026-11-16)

- **US:** the S&P 500 is 5% off its high — still *advance* by the dated phases, *bull* by the 200-day signal. The
  weakness is in the Nasdaq (−13%) and small caps (−10.5%); Minervini's segment filter has been closed since
  2026-09-11.
- **India:** the Nifty 500 has not regained its September 2024 high — a *recovery* phase — and the 200-day signal
  reads *transition*.

## Caveats

- Rows marked **opti** stop at 2021 (no 2022 or 2025 decline); their capture is over four or six declines, not
  six or nine. The full runs for those strategies are queued.
- Each row is one configuration; a different configuration of the same strategy can capture differently.
- Phase returns are arithmetic and annualised over few sessions (US declines are ~11% of sessions), so the
  *capture* column and the per-episode returns are the steadier read.
- The stock universe is today's listed names, so falling years are somewhat flattered (delisted losers missing).
