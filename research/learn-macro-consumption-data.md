# Consumption data — where to find it, sector by sector, in India and the US

**In short**

- Consumption is the largest part of both economies, so its data shows demand before company results do.
- The US publishes almost everything free, monthly and in machine-readable form.
- India's best data comes as monthly press releases and spreadsheets from ministries and industry bodies.
- Paid panels are the only real volume data for packaged goods and restaurants. Company results stand in for them.
- Read India's series year on year: festivals and the monsoon swing single months.

This guide is a map of the sources. It lists, for each key sector, what is measured, who publishes it, how often and how late, and whether it is free. The [growth and business cycle guide](learn-macro-cycle.md) explains how to read these numbers against the cycle.

---

## 1. How to read the tables

Take India's car sales in October. Two numbers appear within days of each other. One counts cars shipped from factories to dealers. The other counts cars actually registered to buyers.

They can disagree for months. Factories ship ahead of a festival, and dealers carry the stock. So each row below says what is counted, not just the sector.

The **Access** column uses three labels:

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Free | The full data can be downloaded at no cost |
| Free headline | A press release gives the main number; the detail is paid |
| Paid | A subscription is needed |

Where a US series is also on FRED, the St. Louis Fed's free database, the row links to it. FRED draws a chart and downloads the full history in one click.

## 4. Reading them well

**Shipped is not sold.** Factory dispatches can rise while registrations fall. That gap is stock piling up at dealers, and it usually ends in discounts or production cuts. Compare the two before reading strength into either.

**Compare with a year earlier in India.** Diwali moves between October and November. A festival month against a quiet month says nothing about trend. US monthly series are seasonally adjusted, so month-on-month changes are meaningful there.

**Dollars are not volume.** US retail sales are counted in dollars, so inflation alone lifts them. Real spending, after inflation, is the better measure of how much people actually bought.

**First prints get revised.** Retail sales, spending and GDP are all revised in later months. A single surprise matters less than the direction over three months.

**Free headlines lag the detail.** For paid panels, the press release often comes weeks after subscribers see the data. Company results are slower still, but they are free and cover the same ground.
